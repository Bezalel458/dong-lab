Three questions I came away with from the heart-brain work, and where I think I could contribute.

A

three questions, after reading closely · tap one

from Cognitive impairment in Asian patients with heart failure: prevalence, biomarkers, clinical correlates, and outcomes  ·  Dong et al., European Journal of Heart Failure 21(5), 2019  ·  the 2022 pilot in Chinese heart failure patients, Frontiers in Cardiovascular Medicine  ·  and projects 10.10 and 10.11 on the NUS Medicine list.

In the 2019 cohort, patients classified as cognitively impaired had MMSE scores inside the normal range while their MoCA separated cleanly: 21.3 against 25.2, p < 0.0001. The impairment did not appear or disappear. The instrument did. MMSE had run out of headroom and MoCA had not.

That is a measurement result as much as a clinical one, and it is the reason the paper mattered. But the same logic keeps going. MoCA is a 30-point ordinal score with its own ceiling, and the follow-up review found the proposed cutoffs had acceptable sensitivity and suboptimal specificity, which is the signature of a scale being asked for more resolution than it has.

So the question I keep arriving at is what sits one rung below MoCA in the same way MoCA sat below MMSE.

the ceiling moved once. that is evidence it can move again.

The 2022 pilot found cognitive impairment in 81% of patients by MoCA below 25, MMSE below 28, or both, with age, NYHA class and atrial fibrillation independently associated.

A prevalence that high is clinically striking and statistically awkward at the same time. A dichotomous label at 81% has spent almost all of its variance before any comparison begins, which caps how strongly it can correlate with anything, including the biomarkers you would most want it to track.

The continuous MoCA score keeps more of that variance than the binary label does. A continuous measure of the brain tissue itself would keep more again. The next tab is that arithmetic, run three ways on the same simulated truth.

prevalence this high is an argument for a finer outcome, not a bigger n.

On one side of the heart-brain link sit NT-proBNP, a continuous assay, plus NYHA class and ejection fraction. On the other side sit MoCA and MMSE. The two halves of the relationship are not measured to the same standard, and the weaker half is the brain.

The mechanism in your own framing is hypoperfusion: an underperforming heart starving brain tissue of oxygenated blood. That is a structural, vascular claim, and it can be imaged directly rather than inferred from a cognitive score downstream of it.

This is the part I have actually done. My project at University Medical Center Groningen was a heart-brain study with imaging at both ends: an automated FSL and Luigi pipeline relating arterial calcification to white-matter injury measured by PSMD, a continuous rater-free marker computed from diffusion MRI, run unattended across a cohort.

my UMCG project was a heart-brain study. this is the same question with a different organ upstream.

B

why this group

Four years of the same problem wearing different clothes: getting a trustworthy number out of a signal that was not built to give one. A dog’s bark. A child’s gaze. A radio wave off a moving body. Arterial calcium against white matter.

Your programme is the first I have found where the question I worked on at Groningen is the clinical question itself, rather than a methods detail inside someone else’s study.

C

the same truth, measured three ways

One simulated cohort, one underlying brain-injury variable, one outcome. The only thing that changes between the three bars is the instrument used to measure the brain. The binary label throws away the most, the 30-point score keeps more, the continuous marker keeps nearly all of it. Nothing about the patients changed. r² = explained variance

at 81% prevalence the binary label keeps the least of the signal.

Simulation, not cohort data. A latent brain-injury variable drives a continuous outcome; the ordinal arm rounds it to a 30-point score and the binary arm thresholds that score at the stated prevalence. Drawn to show what each instrument discards.

D

what the reading buys

If the binary label is the outcome, then a real association can look weak purely because the outcome was dichotomised at a prevalence where almost everyone falls on one side. That is a reporting choice, not a property of the disease.

Keeping MoCA continuous is free. It costs nothing to collect, it is already in the dataset, and it recovers a meaningful share of what the threshold discards.

Adding a continuous imaging marker costs a sequence and a pipeline, and recovers more again. It also moves the measurement to the organ the mechanism is actually about, instead of to a cognitive test several steps downstream of it.

And it changes what the study can claim. A cognitive score tells you a patient is impaired. A white-matter measure tells you something about why, which is what a translational programme from mechanism to trial eventually needs.
